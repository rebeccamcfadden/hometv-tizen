HomeTV check for Samsung TVs

HomeTV is a simple home screen for TVs. Before it goes on a Samsung TV, this check finds out, once, whether it will install and work on that TV. You run it on a Windows laptop, usually during a phone call with the person who sent you this link. It takes about 40 minutes.

What the check does, and doesn't do

  • It looks at the TV: its model, its software version, which of the usual streaming apps are on it, and whether the remote's buttons reach an app.
  • It installs one small app, “HomeTV Check”, on the TV, runs it while you follow its screen, then removes it again, with its settings. Nothing is kept on the TV.
  • It doesn't change any of the TV's settings, apps or accounts, and doesn't install anything on the laptop: it runs from one folder you can delete afterwards.
  • To let the laptop in, you switch on the TV's Developer Mode (Samsung's own switch for app makers). At the end you switch it off again.
  • To install an app, Samsung needs a certificate for that one TV. The tool makes it after you sign in to your Samsung account in the laptop's web browser. The tool never sees your password: you type it into Samsung's own page.
  • At the end it writes a report on the laptop's Desktop for you to send back. What's in the report.

What you need

  • The Samsung TV, switched on, and its remote.
  • A Windows 10 or 11 laptop on the same home network as the TV (the same Wi-Fi, or plugged into the same router). Plugged into power is best.
  • A Samsung account (the one you use on the TV or a Samsung phone is fine). No account? You can make a free one when the sign-in page opens.
  • About 40 minutes. The download is about 16 MB.
  • Optional: a phone on the same Wi-Fi, for one short check.

The steps

  1. Open PowerShell on the laptop. Press the Windows key, type PowerShell, and press Enter. A window with a dark background and a blinking cursor opens.
  2. Copy this command, paste it into PowerShell, and press Enter. Press Copy, then click into the PowerShell window and right-click (that pastes), then press Enter. It downloads the tool, checks that it's exactly the right file, unpacks it into a folder of its own and starts it. 
    $ProgressPreference='SilentlyContinue'; [Net.ServicePointManager]::SecurityProtocol='Tls12'; $z="$env:TEMP\HomeTV-Check-Windows.zip"; Invoke-WebRequest -UseBasicParsing 'https://github.com/rebeccamcfadden/hometv-tizen/releases/download/check-samsung-v1/HomeTV-Check-Windows.zip' -OutFile $z; if ((Get-FileHash $z -Algorithm SHA256).Hash -ne 'not-released-yet') { Write-Host 'This is not the expected file. Stop here and tell the person who sent you the link.' -ForegroundColor Red } else { Expand-Archive $z $env:LOCALAPPDATA -Force; & "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\HomeTV-Check\python\python.exe" "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\HomeTV-Check\app\hometv_check.py" }

    If you'd rather not paste a command: download HomeTV-Check-Windows.zip from the release page, open your Downloads folder, right-click the file, choose Extract All…, then Extract. In the folder that opens, double-click HomeTV-Check, then Start HomeTV Check.cmd. If Windows shows a blue box saying “Windows protected your PC”, choose More info, then Run anyway.

  3. The laptop looks for the TV. The window shows each step in plain words; read it as you go. When it asks, press Enter in that window.
  4. Switch on the TV's Developer Mode. The tool shows the laptop's address (four numbers with dots). On the TV:
    1. Open Apps (press Home, then choose Apps).
    2. On the remote, press 1 2 3 4 5. No number buttons? Press the 123 button (or a colour button) to get numbers on the screen. A Developer mode box appears. (Nothing appears? Close Apps, open it again, and type the numbers slowly.)
    3. Switch it On. Under Host PC IP, type the laptop's address the tool shows. Press OK.
    4. Restart the TV fully: hold the remote's power button until the Samsung logo shows (about 5 seconds). A short press only puts it to sleep, and Developer Mode needs a real restart.
    When the TV is back on, press Enter in the tool's window.
  5. Sign in to Samsung. The tool opens Samsung's sign-in page in your web browser. Sign in (or make a free account there). When the page says “Signed in. You can close this tab.”, go back to the tool's window. The tool then installs “HomeTV Check” and starts it on the TV.
  6. Follow the TV's screen, with the TV's remote: use the arrows and OK to choose. It takes about 20 minutes. What the TV will show. If the TV ever shows something else, open HomeTV Check from Apps and it carries on where it was.
  7. When the TV says “All done”, look at the laptop: it reads the results from the TV by itself, removes the check app, and writes the report. If it asks for a photo, take one of the TV's screen with your phone.
  8. Send the report file to the person who sent you this link, as an attachment to an email or a message. It's on the Desktop: HomeTV-check-report- and the date, ending in .txt (a second one, ending in .json, is the same for a computer: send it too).
  9. Keep the TV's 3 certificate files. At the end, the tool opens the folder they're in: author.p12, distributor.p12 and password.txt. HomeTV's later updates must be signed with the same certificate, so keep them: copy them to a USB stick, or send them to the person who sent you this link. Treat them like a password: don't post them anywhere or give them to anyone else.
  10. Switch Developer Mode off. On the TV: Apps, press 1 2 3 4 5, switch Developer mode Off, OK, then restart the TV fully (hold power). While it's on, its Host PC IP setting is what lets the laptop at that address into the TV.
    If the TV asked you to allow “HomeTV” during the check, remove it too: Settings › General › External Device Manager › Device Connection Manager › Device List › HomeTV › Delete.
    You can delete the tool's folder: in PowerShell, paste this and press Enter. 
    Remove-Item -Recurse -Force "$env:LOCALAPPDATA\HomeTV-Check", "$env:TEMP\HomeTV-Check-Windows.zip"

What the TV will show

  • The Developer mode box (step 4), and the Samsung logo when it restarts.
  • “HomeTV Check”: a dark screen with the steps on the left. It goes through them in order:
    1. Checking this TV by itself, up to a minute. Don't press anything.
    2. Press the RED button, then green, yellow, blue, INFO and the number 5. If nothing happens when you press one, choose “Nothing happens when I press it”.
    3. Can a phone reach the TV? Optional: point a phone's camera at the square code on the TV, or type the address shown into the phone's browser, and say whether a page called “Keys for your TV” opens. Don't type anything into it. No phone? Choose “I don't have a phone to hand”.
    4. Open YouTube and come back: it opens YouTube; press the remote's Back button until you're back, then say how you got back.
    5. The Home button: it opens an app; press the Home button (the house) once and wait 20 seconds. The check app should come back by itself; if not, open it from Apps.
    6. Opening a film or video: it asks YouTube, Netflix and Prime Video (those that are on the TV) to open one title straight away, and asks what opened. The TV may ask whether to allow “HomeTV”: choose Allow (step 10 above removes it again). Nothing is played for long and nothing is bought.
    7. All done, with the results in short lines and a square code.

What's in the report

Only these, and nothing else (the tool drops anything not on this list, on the TV and again on the laptop):

  • The TV: its model code (like UE43TU7000) and board name, the year it's from, its Tizen version, its web engine's version, its screen size in pixels.
  • The laptop: the Windows version, the tool's, Python's and AeroTizen's versions, the date and how many minutes the check took.
  • How it went: whether the laptop found the TV, whether Developer Mode was on and pointed at this laptop (yes or no, not the address), whether the certificate was made, whether the check app installed, started and was removed again.
  • Apps: how many apps the TV has, and for each of these whether it's there (yes or no): Netflix, YouTube, Prime Video, Disney+, HBO Max, Apple TV, Hulu, Peacock, Paramount+, Spotify, Plex, Tubi, Pluto TV. Not the list of your apps.
  • The check app's results: how long it took to start; whether HomeTV's two background helpers answered, and after how long; each remote button (received or not); the phone (yes, no, no phone); opening YouTube and coming back, and the Home button (what happened and how many seconds); for each title tried, whether the app took it, a short error name if not, and your answer (the title, only the app, nothing).

Never in it: the TV's name, serial number or device id, any address (IP or MAC), the Wi-Fi's name, your Samsung account or email, the certificate, or the list of your apps. Open the .txt file in Notepad to see it before you send it.

If something goes wrong

  • “This is not the expected file”: stop, and tell the person who sent you the link.
  • The tool doesn't find the TV: the TV must be on (not asleep), and the laptop on the same Wi-Fi. The tool lets you type the TV's address: TV Settings › General › Network › Network Status › IP Settings.
  • “The TV didn't let the laptop in”: Host PC IP must be exactly the address the tool shows, and the TV must be restarted fully (hold power) after it's set.
  • The sign-in page doesn't open: the tool shows its address; copy it into your browser.
  • Stopped half-way? Run the command again. It doesn't ask you to sign in twice. If “HomeTV Check” is left on the TV: Home › Apps › the gear (Settings) › HomeTV Check › Delete.
